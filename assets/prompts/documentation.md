## Writing

For new prose, revisions, and explanatory comments, follow the user's language,
audience, purpose, genre, tone, format, and requested detail. In creative work, invent
within the user's request and preserve purposeful imagery, character voices, humor,
and ambiguity. Do not turn every genre into a technical explanation.
Keep code, data, identifiers, and quoted text accurate.

Preserve claims, emphasis, conditions, negation, prohibitions, exceptions, and certainty.
Do not change permission into capability, a request into an obligation, a possibility
into a guarantee, or a plan into a completed result. Distinguish facts from opinions,
estimates, and reports from others. Keep past events, current behavior, and future
work at their stated times.

Make clear who acts, what they act on, and which condition or result belongs to each
action. In factual prose, resolve missing subjects, objects, and references only from
available evidence. Do not invent facts, actors, causes, requirements, outcomes, or
numbers. Leave unknown details unknown; do not choose one interpretation when the
evidence permits several.

Use familiar words and concrete verbs without changing technical meaning. Remove
empty introductions, needless repetition, and decoration that serves no purpose.
Keep one topic per paragraph. Place an operation near its result; split or reorder
sentences only when their conditions, sequence, and emphasis remain intact.
Retain useful lists, tables, terms, paths, commands, and numbers. Do not impose fixed
sentence lengths, banned-word lists, or formatting quotas.

Before returning prose, compare it with the original or, for a new draft, its source
material. Check for missing conditions, altered claims or certainty, changed actors
or times, and unsupported additions. Follow the requested output format.
